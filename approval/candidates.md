## 1. Benches from Beach Waste  (environment, impact 9/10)
People have done a wonderful job at Mumbai's Dadar Beach. Over fifteen hundred volunteers joined together here to run a massive cleanup drive. With several hours of hard work, everyone collected eighteen thousand kilograms of waste. A very large portion of this waste removed from the beach was plastic. Now, this entire waste plastic is being given a new life. By recycling this plastic, school benches will be made for underprivileged children. This will give children good seating facilities in classrooms for studying. At the same time, the beach can be kept clean from plastic pollution and filth. Organizers believe that real change comes from segregating waste daily. If we do not throw trash on the beach, the shores will always remain clean. This responsibility of citizens is a very good lesson for both society and the environment. This campaign shows that a big change can be brought with the right mindset.

## 2. Foldable Homes for Flood Victims  (science, impact 9/10)
Severe floods in Chennai snatched away the makeshift homes of many families. Relief arrived immediately after the floods, but building permanent houses takes years. Shreeram Ravichandran, a student at IIT Madras, understood this serious problem deeply. He saw that there is a long wait between relief tents and permanent houses. To ease this very difficulty, he began working with his team. These engineering students developed special foldable and modular buildings. These buildings can be easily set up in places where construction is difficult. So far, their organization has built more than fifteen hundred such modular buildings. This technology helps provide an immediate safe roof to homeless families. With this remarkable ingenuity of the students, a new hope has been sparked in the lives of disaster victims. This step taken in difficult times is truly inspiring for everyone.

## 3. Unique Initiative for a Plastic-Free School  (education, impact 8/10)
In a school in Singapore, students ran a unique campaign against plastic pollution. There, two thousand students used to use seventy-two thousand plastic items every year. To stop this, fifteen to sixteen-year-old children launched a major campaign. They promoted reusable lunch boxes instead of disposable containers. Their effort won the first prize in the Unplastify Challenge 2026. This international program is running in four hundred schools across twenty-five countries. So far, three thousand students together have saved seventy-two tons of plastic. In Chile, children developed an eco-friendly glitter powder. Meanwhile, in Costa Rica, natural loofah was used instead of plastic sponges. Young people in Turkey have researched eco-friendly gloves for hospitals. These small ideas of students are protecting nature worldwide today. The hard work of these children shows that change can begin from school.

## 4. Solar Energy Record in Australia  (environment, impact 8/10)
A huge historic record has been set in the clean energy sector in Australia. There, on Saturday, solar and wind energy generated more than eighty percent of the electricity. During the afternoon, more than half of the electricity was produced solely from rooftops of homes. Across the country, more than forty-three lakh families have installed solar panels. These rooftop panels together met half of the grid's demand on their own. The rest of the electricity was generated from large solar plants, wind turbines, and hydro projects. During this period, the share of fossil fuels like coal and gas remained at just twenty percent. The surplus electricity was securely stored in pumped hydro storage and large batteries. Experts say that several such new records could be set in the coming weeks. This success proves that clean energy can power the entire country.

## 5. Technology to Reduce Noise in Hospitals  (health, impact 8/10)
The noise of machines in hospital wards has always been a major problem. In ICUs, this noise often ranges between fifty to seventy decibels, which is as loud as heavy traffic. Many times, it even reaches one hundred decibels. This is far higher than the thirty-decibel level set by the World Health Organization. In a single shift, staff have to hear hundreds of alarms. Ninety percent of these alarms are such that require no medical intervention. To tackle this problem, the Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust in the United Kingdom took an initiative in the year 2023. They launched a three-year Silent Hospital pilot project. In this, an attempt is being made to route sound like data. The results of its second phase have been quite good. Now, other hospitals in the UK are also preparing to adopt it.

## 6. Protection for Coral Reefs  (science, impact 7/10)
A new way has been found to protect coral reefs from warm ocean water. Scientists placed floating shades over brain coral in the ocean of Florida. These shades were made from PVC frames and nylon mesh. They were floated in the water about twelve inches, or thirty centimeters, above the coral. The natural color of twenty coral colonies under the shades began returning rapidly. Meanwhile, other reefs without shades were losing their color from the harsh sun rays. These shades do not cool the water, but rather block the sun's ultraviolet rays. Nova Southeastern University scientist Karen Neely described this research as very significant. This technique proved effective in providing immediate protection to fragile coral in extreme heat. This successful experiment has raised hopes of saving marine life across the world.

## 7. Clean Gas from Agricultural Waste  (environment, impact 7/10)
A large plant to produce clean energy from agricultural waste has started in Italy. This new biomethane plant has been set up in an area named Grottole in southern Italy. Here, an excellent alternative to natural gas will be produced from crop residues. This plant will produce forty-five gigawatt-hours of renewable energy every year. This electricity and gas will meet the needs of more than three thousand homes. This will lead to a massive reduction of nine thousand tons of carbon dioxide emissions every year. This proper use of waste is a fantastic way to protect the environment from pollution. The company described this entire project as a major step toward a green future. Clean fuel reaching every home from farmers' discarded waste is a very pleasant thing. This model shows that clean energy needs can be met even from waste.
