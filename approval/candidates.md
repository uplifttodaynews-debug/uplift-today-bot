## 1. From Mumbai Beach to Classroom Benches  (environment, impact 9/10)
Very wonderful news about environmental conservation has come from the coast of Mumbai. Around 5 million kilograms of plastic waste goes into the Arabian Sea every year. But to tackle this problem, more than 1,500 volunteers came forward. All these people joined together to completely clean the beach in a single day. They pulled out 18,000 kilograms of plastic waste from the sand by the sea. The bags full of waste were sent directly to a modern recycling center. There, this plastic was washed thoroughly and processed by machines. After this, sturdy benches for school children were made from this recycled plastic. Now these benches are being placed in the classrooms of needy children for studying. This journey from waste to classroom has become an inspiring example for the environment. This solidarity among ordinary people has shaped the future of both our sea and children.

## 2. Solar Waste Truck in Hyderabad  (environment, impact 9/10)
Very unique and positive news about waste management has come from Hyderabad. Akshay Deshpande began researching the waste problem after seeing plastic inside a cow's stomach. He understood the ground reality by interviewing over 780 people. After this, he launched an initiative named SwitchEco Sustainability in Hyderabad. His team developed a 22-foot-long solar-powered truck. This mobile truck visits residential colonies to sort and shred waste right on the spot. Because of this, the waste gets processed before even reaching landfills. So far, this project has processed more than 100,000 kilograms of waste. This campaign has directly impacted the lives of over 400,000 people. Through this, around 180 women have gained new work opportunities. This locally developed solution is helping keep cities clean.

## 3. Caring for Trees in Berlin  (environment, impact 9/10)
Heartwarming news about connecting with nature has come from Germany's capital, Berlin. During extreme summer heat in Berlin, street trees are being saved from drying up. For this, CityLAB Berlin created a digital app called Gieß den Kiez. A complete map of more than 900,000 trees across the city is recorded on this app. The app considers the tree's age and rainfall to show which ones need water. So far, 12,000 citizens have voluntarily joined this campaign. Amanda, who lives in Charlottenburg, gives 20 liters of water to a tree every week. With the help of the app, people also easily locate the city's public water pumps. To protect young saplings, they are shown in a different color on the app. Working alongside municipal staff, local residents are giving trees a new life. This collective care for the environment is a beautiful lesson for every city in the world.

## 4. Silencing Noise in Hospitals  (health, impact 8/10)
Good news regarding patients' comfort has come from hospitals in the United Kingdom. The sound of machines in hospital ICU wards is as loud as heavy traffic. According to the World Health Organization, noise levels should be only 30 decibels for resting. But often, the noise from machines reaches 80 to 100 decibels. In every shift, 90 percent of alarms are those that do not require a doctor. Because of this, both medical staff and patients remain stressed by alarm fatigue. To tackle this problem, Royal Cornwall Hospital started a pilot project. They found a way to route machine sounds directly as data to the right place. With this new system, unnecessary noise in the ward has dropped significantly. Seeing the project's great results, other hospitals across the country are also implementing it. With peace returning to hospitals, patients will now be able to recover much better.

## 5. Eating with Loved Ones Is Healthy  (health, impact 8/10)
An interesting report has emerged regarding the scientific connection between our health and relationships. A study published in the journal Science Advances revealed something very surprising. Scientists at the Hebrew University conducted this special experiment on 108 people. People were given bread to eat both alone and while sitting with a close friend. The research found that blood sugar rises less when eating with loved ones. Eating together also brings blood sugar levels back to normal much faster. Scientists have named this physiological process social physiology. According to researcher Shir Atzil, our relationships directly affect our body's metabolism. When we are close to loved ones, the body experiences less stress. This research shows that eating good food alone is not enough to stay healthy. Time spent with family and friends keeps our entire body healthy.

## 6. Homeless Students Get a Home  (education, impact 8/10)
There is good news for homeless college students in Denver. DePaul USA's DAX Program has opened its first housing for students here. Fifteen students will be able to live in this North Capitol Hill building. This program gives homeless students a safe place to stay for up to two years. Because of this, students can focus on their degrees without worrying about where they will sleep. DAX has partnered with Metropolitan State University of Denver and Regis University. This program started in the year 2015 and has provided housing to 494 students so far. Nykea Harrell also came to Denver for the inauguration of this new center. She told students facing housing difficulties that you are never alone.
