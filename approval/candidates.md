## 1. Flying car for the mountains  (science, impact 9/10)
Very inspiring news has emerged from Almora district of Uttarakhand. Ravi Tamta, a resident of Kaflikhan village, has made a unique invention. Ravi, the son of a mechanic, did not hold any engineering degree. He learned technology from the internet and developed the prototype of a flying car. It used to take hours to reach the hospital on the winding mountain roads. To solve this very problem of medical emergencies, he worked hard for thirteen years. This electric flying vehicle has been named 'Hapida Skynex'. This vehicle can take off directly upwards and also land without a runway. Its first test was conducted at the Army helipad in Almora in the month of August. This electric vehicle successfully flew in the air for about one minute. This technology will prove to be a boon for patients in remote hilly areas.

## 2. Easy travel with a swivel seat  (health, impact 9/10)
Two engineers from Bengaluru have made travel extremely easy for persons with disabilities. Naina Padaki and Anand Kutre started a company named 'True Assistive Technology'. They have created a special swivel car seat named 'TurnPlus'. This seat rotates up to ninety degrees towards the car door. Because of this, passengers can be seated in the car without any trouble. Rashmi Sampat from Bengaluru lives with her sixteen-year-old daughter Deeksha. Deeksha is battling a disease called Spinal Muscular Atrophy. Earlier, seating Deeksha in the car was an exhausting task. With the help of this new seat, their family traveled from Bengaluru to Kanyakumari. They completed this long journey of one thousand kilometers very comfortably. The company has helped more than one thousand people so far. This technology is proving useful for patients with conditions like paralysis and Parkinson's.

## 3. The world's tallest wind turbine  (science, impact 8/10)
The world's tallest wind turbine has been completed in Germany. This massive structure has been erected in the Schipkau area of eastern Germany. The total height of this turbine has reached three hundred and sixty-five meters. After Berlin's TV tower, it is the second tallest structure in Germany. It has been built with the help of the company 'Gicon' and the government agency 'SPRIND'. At such a high altitude, wind speed remains very fast and steady. The company says it can generate twice as much electricity as a conventional turbine. The production capacity of this turbine has been set at three point eight megawatts. For construction at such a height, a telescoping tower was used instead of a crane. The inner tower was raised up and safely locked within the lattice structure. Company CEO Jochen Grossmann called it a major achievement for his team.

## 4. Children's plastic-free campaign  (education, impact 8/10)
Teenagers around the world are stepping forward to tackle the plastic crisis. In a school in Singapore, two thousand students used seventy-two thousand items made of thermocol and other plastics every year. For a solution, they joined the 'Unplastify Challenge'. In this initiative by the Latin American organization Unplastify, fifteen to sixteen-year-old teenagers design solutions. The students of Singapore secured first place by running a campaign for reusable lunch boxes. Over the past eight years, more than three thousand students from four hundred schools across twenty-five countries have joined this campaign. They have helped prevent seventy-two tons of plastic. In Chile, students saved four kilograms of plastic by making biodegradable glitter. In Costa Rica, natural loofahs were adopted instead of plastic sponges. In Turkey, teenagers have explored eco-friendly alternatives to medical equipment like plastic gloves.

## 5. Concrete that grows moss on walls  (environment, impact 7/10)
A startup from the Netherlands has discovered a unique, eco-friendly technology. This Dutch company, named 'Respyre', has created a coating called 'Mosscrete'. It helps grow green moss naturally on concrete walls. This technology is based on research from Delft University of Technology. This moss does not need soil or irrigation to grow. The moss derives its nourishment solely from rainwater and moisture in the air. The fine roots of the moss stick to the surface without damaging the wall. The company has worked on eight different projects in Belgium and the Netherlands. This coating was applied to the wall of a warehouse in Kortrijk, Belgium. In Hoofddorp, Netherlands, it is being tested on a roadside wall. This technology will prove very effective in making bare city walls green.

## 6. A library open even after closing  (education, impact 7/10)
The Wyoming branch of East Central Regional Library in Minnesota, USA, has launched a new initiative called 'Extended Access'. Under this, people can enter even when the library is closed and unstaffed. A man named Michael Sayen works on his novel here on a computer. He shared that even on closed days, one can come here to work or find relief from the heat. Sayen has also found this facility useful for submitting important job applications and court documents. According to the library's executive director, Carla Lydon, this arrangement will allow even those people to use the library who cannot visit during regular hours due to work or family commitments. Such a facility is also running under different names in Utah and Nevada County, California.
