## 1. A car's unique swivel seat  (health, impact 9/10)
Very inspiring news has come from Bengaluru. Here, engineers Naina Padaki and Anand Kutre have done wonderful work. They started a special initiative called 'True Assistive Technology'. Their team has made a swivel seat for cars named 'TurnPlus'. This seat turns ninety degrees towards the door. This makes it very easy to seat any patient in a car. Rashmi Sampath from Bengaluru lives with her sixteen-year-old daughter Deeksha. Deeksha had severe mobility issues, so going out was difficult. With the help of this new seat, they traveled from Bengaluru to Kanyakumari. Throughout this nearly one thousand-kilometer journey, Deeksha remained very comfortable. This technology has helped more than one thousand people across India. Now, going out together has become extremely easy for many families.

## 2. Students reduced plastic waste  (education, impact 9/10)
Schoolchildren across the world are coming forward against plastic pollution. In a school in Singapore, children saw plastic waste in the canteen. There, two thousand children used seventy-two thousand plastic items every year. The students ran a major campaign to stop this problem. They introduced the practice of reusable tiffin boxes. For this brilliant idea, they won first place in the 'Unplastify Challenge'. Four hundred schools from twenty-five countries have joined this global program. Together, around three thousand schoolchildren have saved seventy-two tons of plastic waste. Students in Chile discovered a natural alternative to glittery plastic. Meanwhile, in Costa Rica, children chose natural loofah instead of plastic sponges. Children in Turkey prepared new types of gloves for hospitals. With the wisdom of these young children, our Earth is becoming clean and safe.

## 3. Greenery will grow on concrete walls  (science, impact 8/10)
Science has made a unique discovery for environmental improvement. Respyre, a new startup from the Netherlands, has created the 'Mosscrete' technology. This is a method to grow moss on concrete walls. This technology was developed through research from Delft University of Technology. In this technology, there is no need to put soil or pipes on walls. Fine moss fibers absorb moisture directly from rain and air. With this, both old and new buildings can be made green. Its testing is underway on eight different buildings in the Netherlands and Belgium. In Kortrijk, Belgium, a wall of four hundred and fifty square meters was made green. Meanwhile, an experiment was conducted on a three hundred square meter wall in Hoofddorp, Netherlands. This invention will make cities' blank walls beautiful without any maintenance. Through this, our cities will be able to become full of clean air and natural greenery.

## 4. Library open even when closed  (education, impact 8/10)
There is lovely news related to education and books from Minnesota, USA. Here, the library in the town of Wyoming has started a new facility. This facility has been named 'Extended Access'. Under this, people can go inside even after the library closes. Readers can use computers even when no staff is present there. Local resident Michael Sayen often comes here to work on his days off. Sitting in this quiet place, he works on his novels and important documents. Library director Carla Lydon says this is very useful. Due to busy work hours, many people cannot come during the day. Now, such people can read at any time according to their convenience. Such arrangements are also starting in other states like California and Utah. This initiative is continuously expanding the reach of knowledge in rural areas.

## 5. Grandmother spreading joy through Alpana art  (people, impact 7/10)
A very beautiful and heartwarming story has emerged from Kolkata. Here, seventy-four-year-old Ratnabali Ghosh wakes up at five-thirty in the morning. She creates traditional Bengali Alpana on the streets of Kolkata and outside people's doors. This initiative of hers started to surprise people and brighten their day. Now, it has become a beautiful mission to save Bengal's cultural heritage. She is spreading joy with her art among old houses, elderly couples, and residents of slums. Ratnabali ji is inspired by her grandmother and the artistic legacy of Rabindranath Tagore. She wants to revive this old tradition and pass it on to the new generation. Her effort teaches that along with modernity, preserving our roots is also very important. Truly, traditions are preserved through the desire to bring a smile to someone's face.
