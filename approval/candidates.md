## 1. Heart health will improve with pomegranates and walnuts  (health, impact 9/10)
Scientists at King's College London have made an important discovery regarding heart disease. They found that after eating pomegranates, walnuts, and certain berries, a natural substance called Urolithin A is formed in our bodies. This substance can improve the heart's function by up to eighty percent. This discovery could be especially helpful for a type of heart failure for which treatment options are currently very limited. According to scientists, this natural compound can bring a major improvement to heart health. This substance formed in the body from pomegranates and walnuts could pave a new way for the treatment of heart failure in the future.

## 2. Machine noise silenced in the hospital  (health, impact 8/10)
The UK's Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust has launched a new initiative to reduce noise in hospitals. They started the 'Silent Hospital Pilot Project' in the year 2023. This three-year study focuses on reducing alarm sounds and noise pollution in wards. Here, instead of suppressing sound, it is sent to the right place like data. In hospital ICUs, there is constant noise of fifty to seventy decibels, and at times it reaches eighty to one hundred decibels. Meanwhile, according to the World Health Organization, a sound level of thirty decibels is considered ideal for rest. Hospital staff have to listen to hundreds of alarms during every shift, up to ninety percent of which do not require any medical attention. The results of this project's second phase have been so good that other UK hospitals are also preparing to adopt it.

## 3. Beautiful plates made from areca palm leaves  (environment, impact 8/10)
Several useful ways of utilizing products from the areca nut tree are emerging in India. After harvesting areca nuts, plates are being made from the leaves that fall to the ground, and they are also used as fodder. In Mizoram, Karnataka, and Kerala, researchers and farmers have discovered many better uses for it. In Mizoram, women farmers are making biodegradable plates from leaves fallen on the ground. The Indian Council of Agricultural Research, or ICAR, has helped two groups in Kolasib and Mamit districts by providing machines. Here, women collect the fallen leaves, sort them, and dry them in the sun for at least a week. Then, plates are made using twenty and thirty centimeter dies. Between 2019 and 2022, these two groups together made more than 1.6 million plates. This has also provided tribal women with an additional source of income.

## 4. A unique cultural center for the Deaf  (people, impact 8/10)
Preparations are underway in the UK to build a special center for the Deaf community. This campaign has been named DeafCity Hub. Urban planner and social activist Amanpreet Arnold is leading this initiative. Amanpreet was Deaf herself from childhood and received a cochlear implant at the age of twenty-one. Around eighteen million people in the UK live with hearing loss, but across the entire country, there is not a single permanent cultural center made by and for the Deaf community. DeafCity Hub aims to create a building constructed based on DeafSpace principles. These are architectural guidelines specifically designed for Deaf people. This center will be developed into a national platform for Deaf art, culture, and community life.

## 5. Homeless college students get a safe haven  (education, impact 8/10)
A new housing facility has been opened for homeless college students in Denver, USA. Depaul USA's Dax Program has launched this near the Capitol building. This program provides up to two years of housing for students who are homeless or at risk of homelessness. Here, students can stay safely and focus on their college studies without any worry. This Denver building dates back to the 1890s, where accommodation is arranged for fifteen students. The Dax Program started in the year 2015. The organization runs eleven housing facilities across Chicago, Los Angeles, Philadelphia, and New York, and has provided housing to 494 students so far. In Denver, this initiative has partnered with Metropolitan State University and Regis University. On this occasion, alumna Nikya Harrelle told the students that they are never alone.

## 6. Village peace saved hundreds of vultures  (animals, impact 7/10)
Chirgaon in Raigad district of Maharashtra has set a unique example of saving vultures. Since the year 2002, playing DJs at weddings has been completely banned in the village. Loudspeakers and loud firecrackers have also been completely stopped here during festivals. Rare species of vultures build nests and raise their young in the village trees. Before the noise stopped, only twenty-two vultures were left here. But in the latest count, the number of these vultures has risen to three hundred forty-seven. Nature lover Premsagar Mestri and the villagers worked together to protect the nests of these birds. They have also arranged a quiet environment and safe food for the birds. At a time when the country's vulture population is rapidly declining, this is a major success. Preparations are now underway to declare this area a vulture conservation reserve. This care from the villagers has proved that a little mindfulness can save lives.

## 7. Free music for twenty-one thousand children  (education, impact 7/10)
A non-profit organization in Miami-Dade County, USA, is providing music education to children. The organization named 'Young Musicians Unite' is running free music classes in one hundred thirty-one schools. Around twenty-one thousand students are benefiting from this wonderful program. Teachers believe that because of these music classes, it has become much easier for children to come to school and take an interest in studies. At the same time, they are also able to connect better with their school and community. This initiative has made the school environment even more pleasant and engaging for the students.
