## 1. Won the Battle of Life with Courage  (people, impact 9/10)
The story of thirty-eight-year-old Shijoy Jacob from Mumbai gives us all great courage. Shijoy was born with a foot condition called clubfoot. In his childhood, both his feet were turned inward. His mother Pushpa used to take him to hospitals in Mumbai for treatment. One doctor had even put plaster casts on both his legs. But Shijoy never let this problem get in the way of his dreams. Today, that same Shijoy has cycled hundreds of kilometers across several countries. In the year 2026, he went on a cycling journey from Cambodia to Vietnam with three friends. Throughout this entire journey, they had no support vehicle with them. He carried his own luggage and fixed punctures himself. He has cycled in India, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia. Not only that, he has also completed a fifteen-thousand-foot trek in the Himalayas.

## 2. Mosque Loudspeakers to Give Earthquake Warnings  (science, impact 9/10)
A unique initiative has been taken in Indonesia to save people's lives from natural disasters. Here, mosque loudspeakers will now serve to give earthquake warnings. This brilliant plan was created by a team of researchers from Japan and Indonesia. Dr. Inoue Hiroshi of Kyoto University thought of this during research in Sumatra. There are millions of mosques in Indonesia, and loudspeakers are installed everywhere. This pilot project is being run for five years until the year 2030. These speakers are being connected to a sensor network that measures ground movement. The alarm will sound on the loudspeakers before the strong tremors of an earthquake arrive. Often, mobile networks suddenly fail during major earthquakes. But mosque speakers are managed by local people and are always operational. In some mosques, this system has already been successfully integrated.

## 3. Students Launch Campaign to Eliminate Plastic  (education, impact 9/10)
Schoolchildren around the world are now stepping forward against plastic waste. In a school in Singapore, two thousand students used a heavy amount of plastic every year. Seeing this problem, fifteen to sixteen-year-old children took a big step. They joined a special campaign called the Unplastify Challenge from Latin America. The children of Singapore promoted reusable lunch boxes in the school. For this wonderful effort, they won the first prize in the year 2026. Over the past eight years, four hundred schools from twenty-five countries have joined this movement. More than three thousand students have together prevented seventy-two tons of plastic waste. Students from Chile created a biodegradable glitter to replace plastic. In Costa Rica, children used natural loofahs instead of plastic sponges. Meanwhile, teenagers in Türkiye have researched safer alternatives to medical gloves.

## 4. Safaris Resume in Indravati Tiger Reserve  (environment, impact 8/10)
Good news has arrived for nature lovers from Chhattisgarh's Indravati Tiger Reserve. After a long wait of forty years, safaris are starting here once again. Located in Bastar's Bijapur district, this forest became a tiger reserve in 1983. Due to security issues in that area, visits by tourists were completely stopped. Even the forest department team could not go inside for monitoring. Now, with conditions improving, the government is opening it from November 1, 2026. This vast reserve is spread across approximately two thousand eight hundred square kilometers. Many magnificent wild animals such as tigers, leopards, bears, and deer live safely here. Chhattisgarh's state animal, the rare wild water buffalo, is also found here. According to the forest department, six tigers are recorded here, and a new count is underway. With improved security, officials are now able to patrol the entire forest easily.

## 5. Libraries to Stay Open Even at Night  (education, impact 7/10)
Libraries in rural areas of America have launched a new facility for people. The library in the city of Wyoming, Minnesota, now stays open even after closing hours. East Central Regional Library has named this special program Extended Access. Through this, people can go inside and study even without any staff present. According to library director Carla Lydon, this is very helpful for working people. Those who remain busy during the day can come here at night. Local author Michael Sion sits here to work on his new novel. He says that he fills out job applications and important documents here comfortably. Even on scorching summer days, this place offers relief to people. Such an arrangement has also started in several other states like Utah and California. This small change is making access to books much easier for ordinary people.

## 6. Health Benefits of Japanese Sweet Potato  (health, impact 6/10)
Japanese sweet potato is being widely liked for its distinct flavor and nutrition. Its skin is reddish-purple, and the inside is completely white and creamy. Compared to regular sweet potatoes, it is sweeter and richer in taste. According to dietitian Danielle, it can help provide energy to active people. One serving contains about twenty-six grams of carbs and four grams of fiber. Dietitian Alissa Smolen explains that its fiber can keep the stomach full for a long time. Orange sweet potatoes contain vitamin A, whereas Japanese sweet potatoes have more vitamin C. Vitamin E and special anthocyanin compounds are also found in abundance in it. This is the same nutrient that is also found in blueberries and red cabbage. Experts advise eating it paired with protein and healthy fats. Eating both types of sweet potatoes can provide the body with different nutrients.
