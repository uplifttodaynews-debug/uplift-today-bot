## 1. Youth initiative for plastic freedom  (environment, impact 9/10)
School students around the world are working to tackle the plastic problem. In a school in Singapore, seventy-two thousand plastic items were used every year. Students took part in the Unplastify Challenge and introduced reusable lunchboxes. With this great initiative, they won first place in this competition. This campaign is designed for teenagers aged fifteen to sixteen. Over the past eight years, three thousand students from twenty-five countries have joined it. Together, these children have helped prevent seventy-two tons of plastic waste. Students in Chile prepared a natural alternative to plastic glitter. In Costa Rica, natural loofah was used instead of plastic sponges. In Türkiye, students found an alternative to plastic gloves for health centers. The mindset of these young children is truly making the environment plastic-free.

## 2. Flying car built for the mountains  (science, impact 9/10)
Very inspiring news has emerged from the Almora district of Uttarakhand. Ravi Tamta, a resident of Kafalikhan village, has built a flying car. People used to take hours on winding roads to reach hospitals in the mountains. Seeing this serious problem, Ravi thought of making a flying vehicle. Without any engineering degree, he learned from the internet and worked hard for thirteen years. He developed a prototype of an electric vehicle named Hapida Skynex. This special vehicle can take off and land directly vertically without a runway. In the month of August, its first successful test took place at the Army helipad in Almora. This electric vehicle flew successfully in the air for about one minute. Its main purpose is to deliver immediate help during medical emergencies in hilly areas. This unique invention by Ravi could save many precious lives in the future.

## 3. Tallest wind turbine built in Germany  (science, impact 8/10)
The world's tallest wind turbine is fully ready in Germany. This giant turbine has been installed in Schipkau, eastern Germany. The height of the blade reaches up to three hundred sixty-five meters from the ground. At such a height, wind speed remains very fast and continuous. According to the company, it can generate nearly twice as much electricity as a normal turbine. Its initial capacity has been set at three point eight megawatts. Its rotor hub rotates at an impressive height of three hundred meters above the ground. A special telescopic tower technology was adopted to construct at such a height. It will be installed right above the existing wind farm like a second story. The company's founder, Jochen Grossmann, called it a big dream of his team. Its formal operation starting in November will give new momentum to clean energy.

## 4. Protecting the river with eco-friendly idols  (environment, impact 8/10)
Sculptors from the Kumartuli area of Kolkata have come forward to protect the environment. Sculptor Kaushik Ghosh is making idols using entirely natural materials in his workshop. Leaving aside thermocol and chemical colors, he has returned to his old traditional art. Bamboo, wood, jute rope, and clay are used in the making of these idols. For decoration, he chose the pith of the shola plant and natural colors. Every year after Durga Puja, thousands of idols are immersed in the Hooghly River. In the year two thousand twenty-five, around fifty-two hundred idols were immersed across thirty-four ghats. The river water was heavily polluted by floating waste of thermocol and plastic. Volunteers cleaning the riverbanks faced immense trouble gathering this trash. With Kaushik Ghosh's new thinking, our sacred Hooghly River will remain clean and safe. This initiative shows that both tradition and the environment can be respected together.

## 5. New technique for acrylic glass recycling  (science, impact 8/10)
Scientists at ETH Zurich in Switzerland have achieved a major breakthrough in plastic recycling. Scientists have found a very simple way to make acrylic glass like new again. In the market, it is commonly known as Plexiglas. This transparent glass is found in our home windows, car lights, and furniture. Earlier, recycling this strong material was considered very difficult and expensive. The research team has developed a new and effective low-energy-consuming technology. This technology breaks down acrylic glass almost completely into its original chemicals. Through this, completely high-quality new material can be produced again from the same old material. This new discovery will make the reuse of plastic waste possible at a very low cost. This work by scientists will prove helpful in reducing plastic pollution worldwide.

## 6. Concrete that grows moss on walls  (environment, impact 8/10)
A startup from the Netherlands has discovered a unique technique to turn walls green. This company, named Respyre, has created a special coating material called Mosscrete. This technology is based on research by scientists from Delft University of Technology. With the help of this coating, green moss grows easily on concrete walls. It does not require soil or any watering pipes. The moss absorbs moisture from the air and rain, spreading without damaging the wall. Its successful test was conducted on the wall of a large warehouse in Kortrijk, Belgium. At the same time, it was also applied to a sound barrier wall built in Hoofddorp, Netherlands. Compared to traditional green walls, it incurs no maintenance costs. This discovery will make the plain cement walls of our cities clean and beautiful.

## 7. Japanese mission toward Mars's moon  (science, impact 8/10)
Japan has taken a big and remarkable step in the field of planetary science. On October nineteenth, Japan successfully launched a spacecraft. This craft is heading toward the mysterious moons of Mars. This mission is known as Martian Moons eXploration, or MMX. This spacecraft has departed from Tanegashima Space. The main objective of this mission is to collect soil samples from Mars's moons. After this, the spacecraft will bring these samples back to Earth. This will be the first time in history that a mission brings soil from a Martian moon to Earth. This is an extremely important step in taking planetary science forward.
