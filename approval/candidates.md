## 1. Little Adnan gets a new life  (health, impact 9/10)
Very relieving news has come from a hospital in Dubai. Here, little Adnan had been undergoing a unique treatment since birth. He was suffering severe seizures every thirteen minutes. He had seizures about one hundred and ten times every day. Doctors decided to try a new experimental treatment. Adnan was given this new medicine. As soon as the medicine was given, his seizures stopped completely. This relief continued throughout the day, the whole week, and for weeks to come. The hospital team is extremely happy with this wonderful result. This discovery has become a new ray of hope for many critically ill children. Adnan's family is now breathing a sigh of relief.

## 2. Campaign to save Dharavi's school  (education, impact 9/10)
New Life School in Mumbai's Dharavi has been running for the past forty years. Five hundred and sixty young children come here to study every day. This school did not have ownership rights to its land. Now, amidst Dharavi's new redevelopment, saving this land is very important. Nineteen-year-old college student Daniel Gupta took on this responsibility. Along with his friends, he started a campaign named Dharavi Rising. In the beginning, against a target of twenty lakh, they raised twenty-two lakh rupees. Now their goal is to raise one and a half crore rupees for the school's land. Daniel believes that children's education should not stop under any circumstances. This campaign is giving a new identity to the future of the entire neighborhood.

## 3. Ban on oil drilling in Los Angeles  (environment, impact 9/10)
There is news of a big victory for the environment from the American city of Los Angeles. People living near oil wells there had been troubled for years. Toxic gases and methane were causing an increase in respiratory illnesses among residents. Wendy Miranda and her organization fought against this for ten years. Both Wendy and her mother were battling asthma. Now the city council has imposed a complete ban on oil drilling in the city. An immediate ban has been placed on drilling new wells. All old wells will be permanently shut down over the next twenty years. Los Angeles has become the largest city in America to take such a historic step. This decision will allow millions of people to get clean and healthy air.

## 4. A unique school for refugee children  (education, impact 8/10)
More than seventeen thousand people live in Zimbabwe's Tongogara refugee camp. In the year twenty-twenty, schools there were closed due to the lockdown. Young Prince Bashangezi and his companions did not just sit back and wait. They formed a study group named Tweens for refugee children. Older students began teaching younger children for free right inside the camp. Despite a lack of resources, these students encouraged one another. An organization named Education Matters Africa also fully supported them. This campaign became a means to keep children's education going during difficult times. This effort by Prince proves that if the resolve is strong, anything is possible. Now this group has become the foundation for a better future for the camp's children.

## 5. Support for youth living with autism  (people, impact 8/10)
A special place has been created in Montana, America, for adults living with autism. It is known as Erik's Ranch & Retreats. It was started by Kathryn Nordberg for the future of her son, Erik. This beautiful farm spans across two hundred and thirty acres of land. Horseback riding and trekking are offered to visiting tourists. All these operations are managed by young people with autism. They work here as guides, cooks, and tour leaders. They receive full wages and accommodation for their hard work. Here, they are learning to lead an independent life with confidence. This farm is bringing a huge change to the lives of these young people.

## 6. New plant generating electricity from air  (science, impact 8/10)
Big news regarding clean energy has emerged from western China. The world's largest liquid-air energy storage plant has begun operations there. This plant is a new way to store green energy for long periods. In this process, clean electricity is used to cool air under extreme pressure. The pressure is so high that the air turns into liquid form. When needed, this liquid air is released rapidly. This spins turbines and generates affordable electricity. This method is considered extremely safe for the environment and cost-effective. It will help prevent the wastage of renewable energy. This project is a major step toward providing the world with clean power.

## 7. City walls decorated with moss  (environment, impact 7/10)
In the city of Aachen, Germany, a unique initiative began in the year twenty-twelve. Artist Philipp Wallisfurth wanted to save a one hundred and fifty-year-old tree. Instead of spray paint, he used natural moss. With the help of yogurt, he wrote a message of greenery on a construction wall. People really loved this living art grown on walls. Following this, Philipp developed a new technique to create bio-graffiti using moss. In Cologne as well, he decorated an eight-meter wall with moss. Philipp now lives in Berlin and does this work in several countries. He is turning lifeless concrete walls into lush green art. His art is bringing cities closer to nature.

## 8. Treating blood pressure with Sarpagandha  (health, impact 7/10)
In the year nineteen forty-nine, treating high blood pressure was very difficult. There were no definitive medicines to protect patients' hearts and blood vessels. At KEM Hospital in Bombay, Dr. Rustom Jal Vakil worked on this. He began in-depth research on the traditional Indian plant Sarpagandha. The roots of this plant had been used in India for centuries. Dr. Vakil studied it closely on his patients for ten years. He proved that Sarpagandha could safely lower blood pressure. His hard work earned the Indian herb a place in modern medical science. This discovery opened a new path for treating millions of patients worldwide. Even today, this research is considered a great milestone in the Indian medical world.
