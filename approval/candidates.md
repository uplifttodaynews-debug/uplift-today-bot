## 1. Support for Children of Laborers  (people, impact 9/10)
Every morning, children's smiles blossom at a construction site in Gurugram. Laborers working here leave their children at the Mobile Creches center. Lalita, a care worker at the center, waits for the children starting at eight in the morning. She takes complete care of every child's health and needs. Lalita has been doing this noble work with full dedication for the past twelve years. At the age of twenty, she chose this path after seeing a training center. She funded her education with her salary and also earned a B.Ed. degree. Now she runs the Ashiyana center and bridge courses for children. Because of this center, parents are able to go to work without any worry. The Mobile Creches organization has been transforming the lives of lakhs of children for the past fifty-seven years. Dedicated didis like Lalita are becoming a true inspiration for society.

## 2. Hiware Bazar Defeats Drought  (environment, impact 9/10)
There is a village named Hiware Bazar in the Ahilyanagar district of Maharashtra. According to Sarpanch Popatrao Pawar, in the early nineties, nearly ninety percent of the village's people had migrated out. At that time, only thirteen percent of the land could be irrigated. This area falls in a low-rainfall region and receives around four hundred millimeters of rain annually. After this, Hiware Bazar utilized drought relief funds. Trenches were dug in the village and water conservation structures were built. This improved the agricultural land, and the families who had left returned. In the year 2015, when there was nearly fifty percent less rainfall than normal, crop production here still did not decline. This track record built through relief work continues to prove very useful today.

## 3. Solar Energy Record in Australia  (environment, impact 8/10)
Australia has created history in the field of renewable energy. On September 22, more than eighty percent of the country's electricity was generated from solar and wind. In the afternoon, rooftop solar panels on homes performed wonders. These rooftop panels fulfilled more than half of the total electricity demand on their own. In Australia, solar panels have now been installed in more than 4.3 million homes. The remaining electricity was supplied by large solar parks, wind turbines, and hydro power. During this period, the use of fuels like coal and gas was only twenty percent. Energy Minister Chris Bowen described this as a major victory for clean and affordable electricity. The surplus electricity was stored safely in large batteries and hydro storage. Experts say this record could be broken again in the coming weeks. This success shows how much ordinary people can change the earth together.

## 4. Outpouring of Love for an Elderly Woman  (people, impact 8/10)
Heartwarming news of humanity has emerged from the US state of Tennessee. An elderly woman worked at a movie theater in the city of Maryville. She was forced to work there even after passing the retirement age. A young man visiting the theater noticed her hard work and predicament. He launched an online fundraising campaign to help the elderly woman. People across the internet contributed enthusiastically to this drive. In no time, people raised one hundred and forty-six thousand dollars. Receiving such a large sum filled the elderly woman with joy and astonishment. Now she will be able to leave her job and live a peaceful retired life. This is not the first time young people have taken such a step for senior citizens. The generosity of strangers proved that goodness is still alive in the world.

## 5. Reduced Machine Noise in Hospitals  (health, impact 8/10)
The Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust in the UK has launched a unique initiative. In the year 2023, the trust started the 'Silent Hospital Pilot Project'. It is a three-year study. Its objective is to reduce alarm fatigue and noise in hospitals. Hospital ICUs experience a constant noise level of fifty to seventy decibels, which sometimes reaches up to one hundred decibels. According to the World Health Organization, a sound level of thirty decibels is suitable for rest. The investigation revealed that staff have to hear hundreds of alarms in every shift. In ninety percent of these cases, immediate medical attention is not required. In this project, instead of suppressing the sound, it is transmitted as data. The results of its second phase have been so encouraging that other UK hospitals are also preparing to adopt it.

## 6. New Life for Marine Corals  (environment, impact 8/10)
Scientists in the Florida Keys have conducted an experiment to save corals. In August 2024, divers placed floating shades over twenty colonies of brain coral. These shades were constructed using PVC frames and nylon mesh. They were suspended in the water about thirty centimeters above the coral. These shades were not cooling the water; instead, they were blocking intense sunlight and ultraviolet rays. The twenty corals kept beneath this protection began to regain their color. Meanwhile, the twenty unshaded corals turned pale and faded due to the heat. This study has been published in 'Frontiers in Marine Science'. Karen Neely, lead researcher from Nova Southeastern University, shared that they wanted to evaluate methods to protect corals for the future. She noted that measures like shading can offer short-term protection on a small scale.

## 7. Clean Gas Made from Waste  (environment, impact 7/10)
A European energy developer has launched a large biomethane plant in southern Italy. This plant is located in Grottole, Italy. It will produce an alternative to natural gas from agricultural waste. This will save approximately nine thousand tonnes of carbon dioxide each year. The plant will generate forty-five gigawatt-hours of renewable energy annually. Through this new initiative, more than three thousand households will receive renewable gas.
